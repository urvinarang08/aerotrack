TURN CATEGORIES INTO FEATURES

Encode the data for models

Choose a field from your upload and inspect its category counts and one-hot columns.

🔢

Label encoding

Compact integer labels
High demand→0Normal demand→1

Useful for encoding the target class. Airline IDs are shown as category codes only; the numbers do not mean an airline is ranked above another.

🧩

One hot encoding

Build columns from your uploaded categories
Load a CSV to see its category columns.

Each category becomes its own 1/0 feature. Airport and carrier codes are nominal → one-hot; the high-demand target is binary → 0/1; month is cyclical → sine/cosine. No BTS input has a defensible ordinal order, so none is invented. Less common values are grouped into OTHER for this compact demonstration.

💡

Why encode? It lets a model work with category information while avoiding the false idea that category codes have a numeric order.

TURN ANALYSIS INTO A USEFUL QUESTION

Route-market review watchlist

AeroTrack helps an analyst decide what to investigate next—not what flights to schedule.

🧭

From passenger counts to a review queue

Notebook 09 · end-to-end capstone

Train on 2025, test on 2026 year-to-date, then rank route-month records by predicted passenger count. Each exported row includes the market, observed and predicted passengers, prediction gap, 2025-only high-demand threshold, and a note to verify with richer planning data.

📊 BTS monthly reports→🤖 Holdout-tested estimate→👩‍💼 Human review

Why it matters: it turns model output into a transparent shortlist that can focus an analyst’s attention. Passenger volume alone does not prove unmet demand, so any real decision also needs seats, schedules, fares, bookings, costs, and airport constraints.

2026 holdout MAE 2,865RMSE 6,169R² 0.042Review shortlist 11,421 markets

Coverage is 12 training months plus 7 holdout months. This supports a classroom screening prototype, not a stable multi-year forecast or live flight feed.
